# EnterococciOneHealth

**Comparative genomics and mobile genetic element analysis of *Enterococcus faecium* in a One Health context**

## Overview

This repository contains bioinformatics scripts and workflows developed during my postdoctoral research at the University of Cambridge, in collaboration with the Wellcome Sanger Institute.

The research investigated the genomic diversity, distribution of antimicrobial resistance (AMR) determinants, and contribution of mobile genetic elements (MGEs) to the dissemination of resistance in *Enterococcus faecium* across One Health-associated sources.

Using Oxford Nanopore Technologies (ONT) long-read whole-genome sequencing (WGS) data and comparative genomic approaches, the project aimed to better understand the genetic mechanisms underlying AMR dissemination and the potential connectivity of resistance determinants between bacterial populations from different ecological settings.

## Research Objectives

The main objectives were to:

1. Characterise the genomic diversity of *E. faecium* isolates and thier MGEs from different One Health sources.
2. Identify and compare antimicrobial resistance genes (ARGs) and associated genetic determinants across bacterial genomes.
3. Investigate the distribution and diversity of MGEs, including plasmids and insertion sequences (ISs), and their potential roles in AMR dissemination.
4. Explore associations between resistance determinants, mobile genetic elements, and isolate sources.
5. Develop reproducible computational workflows for large-scale microbial genomic analyses.

## Bioinformatics Approaches

The project involved computational analyses of bacterial whole-genome sequencing data, including:

- **Comparative genomics:** Characterisation and comparison of genomic features across bacterial isolates.
- **AMR profiling:** Identification and distribution analysis of AMR determinants.
- **Mobile genetic element analysis:** Detection and characterisation of plasmids, ISs, and other MGEs.
- **Population genomics:** Investigation of bacterial population structure and lineage-associated genomic variation.
- **Statistical analysis:** Assessment of associations between genomic features, bacterial populations, and sample sources.
- **Data integration and visualisation:** Integration of outputs from multiple bioinformatics tools to investigate patterns of AMR and MGE distribution.

## Repository Contents

This repository will contain selected scripts and workflows developed for the project, including:

- Python scripts for data processing, integration, and statistical analysis.
- Scripts for summarising and visualising comparative genomic results.
- Documentation describing computational workflows and software dependencies.

## Reproducibility

Scripts are provided to document the computational approaches used in this research and to support adaptation for similar microbial genomics projects.
